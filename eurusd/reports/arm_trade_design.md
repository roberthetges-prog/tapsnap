## 1. What exactly was simulated

* Signal: the walk-forward three-signal agreement rule, rebuilt with `committee.build_frame` / `_fold_columns`; the series is asserted identical to `experiments/70_committee.py::agreement_side`. First call 2007.
* Entry at the close of a session on which the rule calls, at that close. One trade at a time; a trade ends only by take-profit, stop or time limit (a flip of the rule does not close it); the next trade opens at the first session close at or after the exit on which the rule calls (a trade that times out may be renewed on the same close).
* Barriers in units of `atr14_pips` (Wilder ATR(14) of the NY-close daily sessions, known at the entry close; the unit of the project's calibrated path probabilities; not the 13-week weekly ATR of `swing.py`).
* Path: hourly bars from the entry close to the close of session entry + limit. Stop fills at the stop or at the bar's open if it opens beyond it; take-profit fills at its level. One hourly bar touching both barriers would be decided by 1-minute bars, stop first if undecidable: this happened in **0 of 147,666** simulated trades (every design at every session, both directions), so the 1-minute data were never needed.
* Costs: 1.2 pips per round turn, plus carry as in `swing.simulate` (EUR overnight rate minus Fed target, actual/360, every night held). Carry averages -0.1 pips a trade for the chosen design (the rule is long 49 % of the time). 0.6 pips adds 0.6 pips to every expectancy and moves no win rate by more than 0.0 points (`trade_design_designs.csv`, `cost_pips` = 0.6).
* Every outcome (real trades, baseline draws, placebo, shuffles) is read from one table holding the result of each design entered at each session close in each direction (`data/processed/trade_design_lookup.parquet`).

## 9. Caveats

* Departures from the declaration, all immaterial: the docstring says calls exist from 2006, the first is in 2007; the table of the four tests for every design (section 6) and the tail table (6b) were added after the run and are descriptive; a timezone bug in the never-used 1-minute path was fixed after a test on made-up cases.

* The agreement rule's inputs carry signs chosen by earlier rounds on this same history; its 2010-26 record is flattered and it is unconfirmed since 2023. Nothing here changes that; this arm only re-expresses the same information as a trade with barriers.
* Wilson intervals treat sequenced trades as independent. Trades of one design do not overlap in time, but the fast designs re-enter the same call several times in a row (up to 28 trades a year from about 5 independent one-month calls), so their intervals are too narrow; the random-entry draws, which keep the spacing, are the better yardstick (`p (draws >= real)`).
* Expectancies are in pips; ATR fell from about 150 pips (2010-11) to about 60-80 (2019-26), so a pip in 2010-17 is not a pip in 2018-26. The ATR-unit and stop-unit columns are scale-free and tell the same story.
* Barrier touches are judged on the bid series for both directions and the whole spread is charged as the round-turn cost; take-profits get no gap improvement; 0 stops of the chosen design were filled through a gap in 2010-26 (10 across all designs).
